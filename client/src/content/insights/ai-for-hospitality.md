Hospitality margins don't leave room for admin staff, so the admin lands on managers who should be on the floor and owners who should be asleep. Enquiries answered at midnight, rotas rebuilt around Thursday's dropout, reviews needing replies, allergen matrices needing updating every time the menu changes. AI won't cook or carry plates — but it's very good at precisely the layer of words and spreadsheets wrapped around the people who do.

## The work AI does well in a restaurant, hotel, or venue

**Event and function enquiries — the money win.** A wedding, a party of thirty, a corporate booking: these are your highest-value enquiries, they arrive around the clock, and the venue that responds first with a real proposal usually wins. AI can answer immediately from your actual packages and calendar, ask the qualifying questions (date, numbers, budget), and draft the proposal for a manager to approve in the morning. For hotels, the same pattern covers group bookings and direct-booking enquiries — every direct conversion is commission an OTA doesn't take.

**Guest questions, answered consistently.** Parking, dogs, dietaries, check-in times, highchairs — the same twenty questions across email, social messages, and your website. Answered instantly from your own information, with anything unusual routed to a human. Out-of-hours coverage alone changes weekend booking numbers.

**Reviews.** Every review answered — promptly, in your voice, and never copy-paste — is one of the highest-leverage marketing habits in hospitality, and one of the first to slip. AI drafts from the actual review content; the manager approves in a daily batch of minutes. The negative ones especially: a calm, specific, non-defensive draft is easier to approve than to write at 11pm.

**Back-office and compliance.** Supplier order sheets from par levels, invoice checking against agreed prices, rota first-drafts from availability and forecast covers (the manager still decides — they know who can't work with whom), and the food-safety paper trail: allergen matrices regenerated when menus change, temperature and cleaning log summaries, EHO-visit paperwork assembled rather than excavated. Allergen information carries legal weight — AI drafts the matrix from your recipes, but a named human checks and owns it, [the same accountability pattern as every regulated corner of this series](/ai-for-smes/).

## What it costs

The [standard ranges](/learn/ai-automation-cost) apply, and hospitality usually enters at the cheap end: enquiry handling and review responses need little integration, so meaningful value starts at assistant-tier money (£15–£60 per user per month). The connected build — AI wired to your booking system and PMS so enquiries check real availability — is the £5,000–£25,000 step, worth it once the manual version has proven the demand.
