A transport office runs on interruptions: where's my delivery, can you quote this load, the POD hasn't come through, the customer's invoice doesn't match the rate card. Every one of those interruptions is text — email, WhatsApp, portal messages — and most of them have answers that already exist in your TMS, your inbox, or a cab somewhere on the M6. That's why logistics is quietly one of the best-fit industries for today's AI: the work is high-volume, rules-based, and constantly repeated.

## The work AI does well in a haulage or logistics firm

**"Where's my delivery?"** answered without a phone call. The status exists in your transport management system; an assistant connected to it can answer customer chasers instantly and accurately, update portals, and flag the exceptions — the late runner, the failed delivery — to a human who can actually do something about them. Traffic office time goes to solving problems instead of reporting statuses.

**POD chasing and invoicing.** Unbilled work waiting on paperwork is the sector's classic cash-flow leak. AI can chase outstanding PODs systematically, extract the details from the scans and photos that come back, match them to jobs, and stage the invoices — so billing runs on completion, not on whenever the paperwork mountain gets climbed. The same extraction works in reverse on supplier and subcontractor invoices: matched against agreed rates, mismatches flagged, the 90% that reconcile cleanly passed through.

**Quoting loads.** Rate judgement stays with your traffic planners — margins, backloads, and awkward customers are knowledge, not arithmetic. What AI removes is the transcription around it: enquiry details extracted into your quoting format, distances and accessorials pre-filled, the quote document drafted for a planner to price and send. Response speed wins freight the same way it [wins construction work](/insights/ai-for-construction-trades).

**Compliance admin.** Driver document chasing (licences, CPC cards, insurance), defect report follow-ups, and audit paperwork assembled steadily instead of in a panic before the DVSA visit. The boundary: tachograph analysis and drivers' hours enforcement belong in your specialist compliance systems — AI assists the chasing and the paperwork *around* the regime; it is not the regime.

## What it costs

The [standard SME ranges](/learn/ai-automation-cost) hold: assistants at £15–£60 per user per month, connected builds £5,000–£25,000. Logistics builds usually sit mid-range — TMS platforms vary in connectability the way [manufacturers' ERPs do](/insights/ai-for-manufacturers) — and the POD-to-invoice automation tends to justify itself on cash-flow improvement before you even count the hours.

## Where to start

POD chasing and invoicing, almost always: it has a cash number attached, the volume is daily, and nothing about it is safety-critical. Status-chaser handling second once the TMS connection exists. Quoting third. And the [general sequencing rules](/ai-for-smes/) apply unchanged — one process, measured, then the next.
